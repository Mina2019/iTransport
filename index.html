Transportation-engineer interview sprint

Use these prompts to practise a clear, structured answer: define the issue, identify data and stakeholders, compare alternatives, then explain how you would measure the result.

How do you approach a transportation problem?

Start with the decision to be made and the system context. Confirm the users, location, data, constraints, safety risks, policy objectives, and stakeholders. Develop practical alternatives, evaluate trade-offs, recommend an implementable action, and state how success will be monitored.

How do you balance mobility and safety?

Use a Safe System mindset. Consider all users and exposure, not just vehicle delay. Match speed and design to the road function and surrounding context; then measure safety and operations before and after any change.

What data would you collect before changing an intersection?

Typical inputs include turning movements, pedestrian and cyclist activity, queues, delay, travel times, collision history, speeds, signal timing, geometry, land use, transit activity, and field observations at relevant times.

How would you explain a traffic-impact assessment?

It estimates how a development could affect the transportation network. Define the scope with the jurisdiction; use defensible assumptions for trips, distribution, mode share, and future conditions; test operations and safety; then propose feasible mitigations and explain residual impacts.

How do land use and transportation influence each other?

Land use shapes trip origins, destinations, timing, and viable travel modes. Transportation investments also affect access and development patterns. Good planning considers them together, with attention to walking, cycling, transit, freight, parking, and equity.

How would you prioritize a pavement program?

Use sound asset inventory and condition data, traffic importance, risk, treatment timing, drainage, available budget, and life-cycle cost. The aim is the right treatment at the right time, rather than waiting until all assets need expensive reconstruction.
